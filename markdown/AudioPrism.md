# Audio Prism

The audio spectrum becomes the light spectrum. On a dark optical bench, a white beam
crosses a hazy studio and refracts through a glass prism (real Snell's law, with
exaggerated dispersion). The light leaves the glass white where the colours still
overlap, separates through the smoke, and lands on a card as a live spectrograph:
each wavelength's brightness is a frequency band (red = bass … violet = treble,
tilt-compensated), and bands that dip below their neighbours show as dark absorption
lines. A glossy floor reflects the scene and catches soft pools of coloured light.

**Audio mapping**
- **Spectrum** — brightness of each wavelength in the fan and on the card.
- **Kick** — the fan visibly opens, the card fills and the room's bounce light breathes. Kicks are detected relative to the track's own running bass level, so heavily compressed masters still read.

**Events** — every ~12 s an epoch may swing the bench (the fan shifts and narrows) or
eclipse the beam (it dims to a thin line with a small spectrum on the card, then
blooms back). With no audio it rests in that eclipse look.

**Parameters**
- **Beam Power** — overall light.
- **Dispersion** — how widely the colours spread (0 = a single white beam).
- **Haze** — smoke scatter, key-light shafts, bounce and lamp spill.
- **Glass** — prism rim, fringes, sheen, internal glow and pedestal caustic.
- **Floor Gloss** — strength and sharpness of the floor reflection.
- **Hue Shift** — rotates the palette.
- **Audio Sensitivity** — gain on the spectrum and the kick detector.
- **Brightness** — exposure into the soft tone-mapping.
